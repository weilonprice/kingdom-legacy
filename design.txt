================================================================================
KINGDOM LEGACY — GAME DESIGN OUTLINE
================================================================================
Engine: Godot 4.7 (GDScript)        Art: 32x32 pixel art (PixelLab)
View: Top-down 2D                   Status: Playable prototype (M0-M6)
Last updated: 2026-09-22


1. PITCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Grow a medieval hamlet into a thriving kingdom, then defend it. A deep city-
building sim in the spirit of SimCity / Cities: Skylines / Banished, where every
villager is a visible person hauling goods along the roads you lay, and where
goblins, orcs and trolls raid in escalating waves that test the town you built.


2. PILLARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - A LIVING TOWN: every citizen is an agent you can watch. Goods physically
    move. Bottlenecks are visible, not just numbers.
  - ROADS AND LAYOUT MATTER: distance, road networks and placement drive
    efficiency. A well-planned town outperforms a sprawling one.
  - THE ECONOMY FEEDS THE ARMY: troops, walls and weapons come from supply
    chains. A strong economy is your best defense.
  - EARNED ESCALATION: raids grow with your kingdom. Expansion is always a
    risk/reward decision.


3. CORE LOOP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Build housing -> villagers arrive -> assign them to jobs (automatic)
    -> gather raw resources -> process into goods -> satisfy needs
    -> population grows & settlement tier rises -> unlock buildings/research
    -> raids grow stronger -> build defenses & train troops -> survive -> repeat


4. WIN / LOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Goal-based. Default scenario goals (tunable per map):
    - WIN:  Reach the Kingdom tier AND survive the final great raid.
            [implemented M8a] Reaching Kingdom wakes the Dragon: the next
            raid comes 3 minutes later, is 1.5x the usual size and is led by
            the Dragon. Killing it and clearing the raid shows the victory
            screen.
    - LOSE: The Keep is destroyed, OR population falls to zero.
            [implemented: end screen with New Game and Main Menu]
  Difficulty (main menu): Easy (raids 70%, first raid at 10 min, extra
  supplies), Normal, Hard (raids 135%, first raid at 5 min).
  Future: scenario-specific goals (survive N raids, reach X population,
  build a Cathedral, etc.).


5. THE MAP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Procedurally generated each game (128x128 tiles, seed shown for sharing).
  - Terrain: Water, Sand, Grass, Forest, Stone. Planned: Hills, Iron/Gold ore,
    Fertile soil, Rivers, Swamp.
  - Resource tiles deplete (forests -> grass). Planned: forest regrowth,
    forester building to replant.
  - Start area is guaranteed clear with forest and stone nearby.
  - Raid spawn points sit at the map edges. Two goblin lairs are placed in
    the wilds (30+ tiles from the Keep); each adds 3 goblins to every raid
    until the player's troops destroy it (+150 gold bounty).


6. CITIZENS (AGENT SIMULATION)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Each villager has: a home, an optional job, a carried item, and a state.
  - Spawn: move into houses with free space and road access.
  - Jobs: auto-assigned to the nearest open workplace.
  - Work: walk to resource / workplace, produce, drop goods at the
    workplace pile; haulers (or the worker, when the pile is full) carry
    them to storage.
  - Movement: A* pathfinding. Roads are ~1.6x faster; forests slower.

  Needs (implemented M5; see scripts/data/need_defs.gd):
    Food      residents fed                      +10 / -30 if not
    Water     within 6 tiles of a Well           +10 / -10 if not
    Religion  within 10 tiles of a staffed Chapel  +10
    Market    within 10 tiles of a staffed Market  +10
    Tavern    within 10 tiles of a staffed Tavern  +10 (Town tier)
  Happiness = 50 + needs + tax effect (None +15 ... Harsh -25), 0-100.
  House levels: Cottage (food) -> Townhouse (+water, religion) -> Manor
  (+market, tavern). Each level: +2 residents, more tax.
  Effects: 70+ works 15% faster; <40 works 25% slower; <35 nobody moves
  in; <25 pays no tax and loses a resident every 45s.
  Day/night (M5b): 150s day, 50s night. Villagers sleep at home at night;
  guards, scholars and service workers stay at their posts.
  Planned: aging, births.


7. ECONOMY & SUPPLY CHAINS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Raw resources:  Wood, Stone, Wheat, Fish, Game, Iron ore, Clay, Wool, Gold ore
  Storage:        Keep (starts, + gold treasury), Stockpile (materials),
                  Granary (food), Warehouse (both, Town); Carter's Yard haulers

  Chains (-> = building transforms input to output):
    Wood         Woodcutter                          [BUILT]
    Stone        Quarry                              [BUILT]
    Bread        Farm -> Wheat -> Mill -> Flour -> Bakery -> Bread   [BUILT]
    Fish         Fisher's Hut (coast) -> Fish                       [BUILT]
    Meat         Hunter's Lodge -> Game -> Butcher -> Meat
    Planks       Wood -> Sawmill -> Planks
    Iron         Iron Mine (rocks) -> Iron                          [BUILT]
    Weapons      Iron -> Smithy -> Weapons                          [BUILT]
    Armor        Iron -> Armory -> Armor                            [BUILT]
    Tools        Iron -> Blacksmith -> Tools
    Bows         Wood -> Fletcher -> Bows / Arrows
    Cloth        Sheep Farm -> Wool -> Weaver -> Cloth
    Bricks       Clay Pit -> Clay -> Kiln -> Bricks
    Gold         Market / Taxes on houses; Gold Mine -> Mint

  Storage (M5b): goods sit in specific buildings. Workplaces keep a pile of
  finished goods (16 max); storage buildings hold their own inventory; gold
  is the Keep's treasury. Producers fetch inputs from storage or straight
  from another workplace's pile. Haulers (Carter's Yard) empty piles into
  storage and supply producers; without them, workers self-haul full piles.
  Gold: taxes each minute = residents x rate x house level multiplier (1 /
  1.5 / 2); set the rate on the Keep. Spent on troop upkeep, training and
  research.


8. SETTLEMENT TIERS & RESEARCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Implemented (M4, needs-based requirements added in M5) — tiers advance
  automatically when requirements are met.

  Tier        Requirement                        Unlocks
  ----------  ---------------------------------  ------------------------------
  Hamlet      Start                              House, Well, Woodcutter,
                                                 Quarry, Farm, Fisher, Mill,
                                                 Bakery, Stockpile, Granary,
                                                 Guard Tower
  Village     15 pop, Well, Granary              Barracks (Militia, Spearmen),
                                                 Scholar's Hall, Stone House,
                                                 Chapel, Market, 5 research
  Town        35 pop, 2 raids survived,          Stone Tower, Warehouse,
              Barracks, Scholar's Hall,          Tavern, Archers, 2 research
              4 Townhouses
  City        70 pop, 5 raids, 4 research,       (more in later milestones)
              Warehouse, Stone Tower,
              6 Manors, happiness 60
  Kingdom     120 pop, 8 raids, 6 research,      Final raid (M8)
              happiness 65

  The Keep gains HP and storage each tier (Keep -> Castle -> Citadel).
  Raids grow by +2 goblins and +1 brute per tier.

  Research (Scholar's Hall; gold up front, time scales with scholars):
    Village: Crop Rotation (+50% farm yield), Sharp Tools (gathering 30%
             faster), Wheelbarrows (+20% villager speed), Fletching (towers
             +2 / archers +1 range), Ledgers (+25% storage)
    Town:    Tempered Steel (+25% troop damage), Masonry (+50% building HP)


9. DEFENSE & COMBAT (HYBRID)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Troops auto-defend by default; player may take direct control of squads.

  Defensive structures:
    Built:   Guard Tower (Hamlet; range 7), Stone Tower (Town; range 9,
             700 HP). Both need a villager on watch.
             Palisade (Hamlet; 4 wood/segment, 250 HP), Stone Wall (Town;
             6 stone/segment, 800 HP, fireproof), Gate (Hamlet; lets
             villagers and troops through, can sit on a road). Walls are
             dragged like roads and join up with their neighbours.
             Wall Tower (Town; 30 stone + 10 wood, 700 HP, range 7): joins
             walls and shoots on its own, no guard needed.
    Planned: Moat, Spike trap.

  Troops (trained at the Barracks, Village tier; each uses up a villager):
    Built:   Militia (10 gold), Spearman (25 gold + 1 weapons; 2.5x damage
             vs large enemies), Archer (Town; 20 gold + 10 wood + 1 weapons;
             range 5), Knight (City; 60 gold + 1 weapons + 1 armor; 180 HP,
             1.5x vs large). Upkeep 1-4 gold/min; troops eat; unpaid troops
             desert.
    Planned: Swordsman, cavalry.

  Behavior:
    - AUTO: squads hold their rally flag and attack raiders within 8 tiles
      of it or within 12 tiles of the Keep.
    - MANUAL: select a squad, right-click to move or attack; G releases it
      back to AUTO guarding where it stands.
  Villagers hide at home when raiders come within 10 tiles. Call to Arms
  (button under the raid banner, or C) makes them fight nearby raiders with
  pitchforks instead (40 HP while armed, 6 damage a second) until the raid
  ends or they stand down; below 40% HP they break off and hide. Work stops
  while they fight.
  - Lairs (2500 HP): order a squad to attack a lair (right-click it). The
    lair sends out a goblin brute every 8s (3 alive at most) while troops
    are within 9 tiles; troops fight
    the guards first, then the den. Dark arrows at the screen edge point to
    lairs off-screen.
  Troops heal near their barracks; dead troops are lost citizens.


10. ENEMIES (MONSTER RAIDS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Raids (implemented M2): first at ~7 min, then every 5 min, with a 30s
  warning naming the direction and an arrow to off-screen raiders.
  Strength: 2 + pop/6 + raids survived + 2 per tier goblins; brutes from
  the 2nd raid on (+1 per tier).

  Enemy types:
    Goblin          Fast, weak, steals from a storehouse and flees  [BUILT]
    Goblin Brute    Large; smashes buildings, then the Keep          [BUILT]
    Goblin Archer   Ranged harassment                        (planned, early)
    Orc Warrior     Tough melee, smashes buildings, starts fires [BUILT]
    Orc Shaman      Heals nearby raiders                     [BUILT]
    Troll           Very slow, huge HP, smashes walls        [BUILT]
    Wolf Riders     Fast flankers, target outlying farms     [BUILT]
    Dragon          Final boss (3000 HP) [BUILT]: flies straight over walls
                    to the nearest building and breathes fire (45 damage in
                    ~2 tiles, sets buildings alight). Only towers and archers
                    hit it in the air; it lands for 9s every 24s so melee
                    troops can strike. At 50% HP it summons 4 brutes and 3
                    orcs; below 25% it enrages (faster, breathes more often).

  Enemy goals (varies by type): loot storage, burn buildings, kill villagers,
  reach the Keep. Raiders smash through walls and gates when those are the
  cheapest way in. Orcs can set wooden buildings on fire: fire eats 3% of HP
  a second, spreads to flammable neighbours, and a Well covering the
  building puts it out after 8 seconds.
  Raid mix (M7): orcs from the 2nd raid, a shaman per 3 orcs, wolf riders
  from Town tier, trolls from the 5th raid or City tier.


11. CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pan camera        WASD / Arrow keys / Middle-mouse drag
  Zoom              Mouse wheel (zooms to cursor)
  Build             R Road, Tab cycles categories, 1-9 picks a building
  Select            Left-click a building to inspect it
  Demolish          X (50% refund)
  Cancel            Right-click / Esc
  Pause / speed     Space / top-bar buttons (1x, 2x, 4x)
  Squads            Click a troop / drag a box to select; right-click to
                    move or attack; G = guard here; Ctrl+1-9 = squad N
  Tier panel        T (or the ⚑ button in the top bar)
  Overlays          O cycles happiness / services / road access
  Tax rate          Click the Keep
  Debug             F9 calls a raid now (30s warning)


12. UI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Top bar: settlement tier (click for the tier panel), materials and food
    with storage use, gold with net income, population / jobs / happiness,
    raid countdown, day/night clock, speed buttons. Labels truncate with
    their full text in a tooltip.
  - Bottom bar: Road and Demolish tools, category tabs (Housing, Resources,
    Industry, Food, Storage, Defense, Civic) and the buildings in the current tab;
    locked ones show a lock and their requirement.
  - Building panel (click a building): status, workers and what each is
    doing, residents, needs, stored goods, workplace pile, recipe; training
    at the Barracks, research at the Scholar's Hall, tax rate on the Keep.
  - Squad panel, tier panel (T), raid banner and edge arrow, overlays (O),
    hover info, notifications, end screen (defeat or victory).
  - Main menu (M8a): map seed, difficulty, tutorial hints on/off.
  - Tutorial hints (M8a): 11 step-by-step goals, top-left, each ticks off
    when done (lay a road, house, woodcutter, food, tower, Village, troops,
    Town, City, Kingdom, the Dragon); ✕ hides them.
  - Planned: minimap, settings, save/load (M8b).


13. ART DIRECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - 32x32 top-down pixel art, warm medieval palette, readable when zoomed out.
  - LOCKED STYLE (art test, see docs/style_test.png), implemented in M6:
      - Buildings in 3/4 view (front wall + door visible), black outlines,
        basic shading, warm thatch/timber tones. All 21 buildings were made
        with PixelLab Pro Flash using the approved cottage as style reference.
      - Terrain: muted olive grass; sand, water, stone, forest and dirt roads
        blend into it through Wang corner tilesets (dual-grid rendering).
      - Characters ~16x31 px figures on a 48x48 canvas (brute 68x68), black
        outline, 4 directions, 4-frame walk cycles.
      - HUD: PixelLab pixel serif font, dark wood panels with gold trim,
        small resource icons.
  - Every art file is optional: if one is missing the game draws the old
    placeholder shape for it.
  - PixelLab budget: Tier 1 subscription (2000 generations per cycle). Real
    costs: Pro Flash building 5, tileset ~3-4, character 1, walk animation
    1 per direction, font 25, UI kit 20, small image 1.


14. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  scripts/main.gd                      Wires systems together; --seed=N
  scripts/autoload/game_state.gd       Resource totals (front for Stock),
                                       population, happiness, tax rate,
                                       research modifiers, speed, game over
  scripts/data/                        Data tables: building, item, enemy,
                                       unit, tier, research, need defs
  scripts/world/terrain.gd             Terrain types, rules, tileset atlas
  scripts/world/world_map.gd           Grid, generation, roads, fields,
                                       placement, A*, enemy registry
  scripts/world/building.gd            Placed building: health, towers,
                                       inventory, workplace piles, home data
  scripts/agents/villager.gd           Citizen agent: work loops, hauling,
                                       hiding, sleeping
  scripts/agents/enemy.gd              Raider agent (thief / wrecker)
  scripts/agents/troop.gd              Soldier agent (auto / manual)
  scripts/combat/                      Health, projectiles
  scripts/systems/citizen_manager.gd   Spawning, jobs, food, drafting
  scripts/systems/raid_director.gd     Raid schedule, spawning, repair
  scripts/systems/military.gd          Training, squads, upkeep
  scripts/systems/squad.gd             Squad modes, orders, formation
  scripts/systems/progression.gd       Settlement tiers, unlocks, Keep upgrade
  scripts/systems/research.gd          Research queue, modifiers
  scripts/systems/needs.gd             Needs, happiness, house levels, taxes
  scripts/systems/stock.gd             Per-building inventories, sources
  scripts/systems/day_night.gd         Day/night cycle and world tint
  scripts/art/art.gd                   Art loader, Wang tilesets, characters
  scripts/world/terrain_renderer.gd    Dual-grid terrain drawing
  scripts/controllers/                 Camera, build tools, unit control
  scripts/ui/                          HUD, building/squad/tier panels,
                                       overlays, raid arrow, game over, theme
  tools/verify/                        Real-input verification harness
  .claude/skills/verify-kingdom-legacy Agent guide + feature map for it

  Notes:
    - Pathfinding: AStarGrid2D, road weight 1.0, terrain 2.0–3.5.
    - Goods live in per-building inventories (systems/stock.gd); GameState
      caches kingdom totals for the HUD.
    - Performance target: 300+ agents; move to flow fields / path caching /
      staggered updates if needed.
    - Save/load planned once core systems stabilize.
